AgriPath Privacy Policy
Updated November 5, 2026

Contact: hello@agripath.app

AgriPath records precise GPS locations, timestamps, GPS accuracy, spray coverage, field boundaries, equipment settings, the spray notes you enter, the applicator details you enter for spray records and the photos you add to a job. Active jobs and completed records are stored in SQLite on your device; preferences and recent weather are stored locally, and each spray job keeps the weather readings recorded for it. AgriPath has no account system, advertising or analytics SDK. We do not upload your spray history or field library. The network requests AgriPath makes are described below: optional weather, maps, app updates, and problem reports you choose to send.

Background location is used only for recording you start and optional wind alerts you enable. Pausing recording stops adding points. The operating system can stop background work; after an interruption, recorded points remain available, but missing locations cannot be recovered. Wind jobs may read a recent location while alerts are enabled. Disable wind alerts to stop those checks. While a spray job is recording with weather on, AgriPath also requests weather for the job's location when the job starts, about every 15 minutes while it records (including in the background) and when you tap Finish Job; turn weather off in Settings to stop these requests.

When you enable weather, AgriPath sends coordinates rounded to two decimal places to the AgriPath weather gateway hosted on Cloudflare. The gateway requests forecasts for those rounded coordinates from the US National Weather Service for US locations, and from WeatherAPI.com for locations in other countries or if the National Weather Service is unavailable. Weather outside the US has not been field tested and may be unavailable in some regions. The gateway does not store or log your coordinates or spray records: it keeps no request logs, uses a hash of your IP address (or its network prefix) only for temporary rate limiting, and briefly caches forecasts by rounded coordinates. Cloudflare receives your IP address and network metadata, and the weather providers receive the gateway's request, under their own policies. Weather caching on your device is replaced by later forecasts. Readings recorded with a spray job (wind speed, gusts, direction, temperature and humidity, with their time and forecast provider, and any you type in by hand) are stored with that job on your device until you delete it, and are never uploaded. Weather is optional and needs internet access.

Google Maps on Android and Apple Maps on iOS supply the map and satellite imagery. These map services may process device, network, diagnostic and location-related information under their providers' privacy policies. Tracking-only map mode hides satellite imagery; on iOS, a standard Apple Maps base map is used and cached map details may remain visible. This mode does not remove the platform's map service from the app.

App updates: each time AgriPath starts with internet access, when you return to it after an hour or more (or after 10 minutes if the last check could not connect), and when you tap Check for updates in Settings, it asks Expo (EAS Update, Expo's update service for apps built with Expo) whether a newer version of the app's code is available, and downloads it if so; a downloaded update takes effect when you tap Restart now or the next time the app starts. AgriPath never restarts on its own. The request includes a random installation ID that Expo's update software creates on first launch and keeps on the device (it is not your name, an account or a phone hardware identifier, and it is replaced if you clear app storage or reinstall), the platform (Android or iOS), the app's runtime version and release channel, the IDs of the app code currently installed and built in, and the IDs of any updates that failed to start. If the update software itself failed on an earlier start, an excerpt (up to 1,024 characters) of the update system's error log is included. Expo receives your IP address and network metadata with the request. The request does not include your location, spray records, field boundaries, equipment or notes. Checking never delays the app or recording; offline, AgriPath runs the code it already has.

Exports include coverage, date, acreage, boom width, field and spray notes. The spray record PDF also includes the applicator name, certification or license number and business name you enter (in Settings or for one job), the products, the job's weather readings and a coverage map drawn from your recorded GPS data and up to four of the job's photos; it is made on your device without internet access. Only export to destinations you trust. The receiving service controls its copy. Temporary export files are stored in the device cache.

Photos: you can add up to 10 photos to a spray job. AgriPath asks for camera access only when you tap Take photo, and uses your phone's camera app to take the picture. Choose photo opens your phone's photo picker, so AgriPath receives only the photos you pick and has no access to the rest of your library. Each photo is resized and saved again without its location or other camera details, and is kept in AgriPath's own storage on your device with its job, never in your gallery, and is never uploaded. A photo leaves your device only when you share it or share a spray record that includes it. Deleting a job deletes its photos. On Android, AgriPath turns off cloud backup, so photos are not backed up; on iPhone they are part of the device's own backup. Photos are lost if you uninstall AgriPath or clear its storage.

Report a problem: AgriPath keeps a short log of recent errors on your device, the newest 50 entries, each with a time, the part of the app involved and a short technical message from which locations, links, file paths and quoted text are removed. Field names, products, rates and job notes are never logged. The log is never sent automatically. If you tap Report a problem in Settings, AgriPath opens your own email app (or, if none opens, your phone's share sheet) with a message to hello@agripath.app containing that log plus the app version and build, the update ID, your phone's make, model and Android or iOS version, your unit and weather settings, and whether a job is active. You see the whole message and can edit or delete any of it before you choose to send it, through your own email provider. We use reports only to diagnose and fix problems.

You control retention: delete completed sessions (with their photos) and fields in the app, or discard the active job. Clear app storage or uninstall to remove all local data, including photos, preferences, the error log and export cache. Android cloud backup is disabled. Exported copies held elsewhere are not deleted by these actions.

We do not sell personal information. Service providers process requests to provide maps, optional weather and app updates. Contact hello@agripath.app for privacy questions or requests. We cannot recover records that exist only on your device.

This app is intended for adult agricultural operators. Phone GPS and forecast data are estimates, not precision guidance or an on-site wind measurement. Check GPS quality, local conditions and product instructions before spraying.

Provider policies: Google, Apple, Cloudflare, Expo, National Weather Service, WeatherAPI.com, Kit.

Newsletter

This section covers the email newsletter on agripath.app, not the app. If you sign up for AgriPath updates, your email address is sent to Kit (kit.com), the service that keeps our mailing list and sends the emails. Kit also receives your IP address and browser details when you sign up, and records whether each email is opened and which links are clicked. Nothing is sent until you confirm by email. Every email has an unsubscribe link, or email hello@agripath.app and we will remove you. We use the list only to send AgriPath news and never sell or share it. The AgriPath app does not send your email address or any app data to Kit.
